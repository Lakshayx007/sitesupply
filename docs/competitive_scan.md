# Competitive scan

This compares **categories** of how site teams track deliveries today, not specific companies. No market-size numbers.

| Category | What it does well | What it misses for a site engineer | Where SiteSupply fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phone calls and WhatsApp** | Fast, free, everyone already uses it, personal relationships with vendors | Only works after someone notices a problem; nothing is recorded; depends on the vendor picking up | SiteSupply sends the warning before anyone has to call, and drafts the WhatsApp message with the reasons |
| **Excel or Google Sheets trackers** | Flexible, cheap, easy to share with the office | Updated by hand, usually after the fact; no live dispatch status; no early warning | SiteSupply keeps the tracker live and adds a risk score and alerts on top of it |
| **Generic procurement software** | Purchase orders, approvals, invoices, spend reports | Built for the office, not the site; tracks paperwork, not whether the truck left; reliability is rarely shown next to price | SiteSupply focuses on delivery risk and puts adjusted reliability next to price |
| **Logistics GPS-tracking tools** | Live truck location and ETA once the truck is moving | Need devices or driver apps; don't know the vendor's track record or rush orders; say nothing before the truck leaves | SiteSupply warns before dispatch from order signals; GPS joins as a v2 signal for delays on the road |

## Takeaways
- Nobody in these categories combines **pre-dispatch signals** (vendor record, distance, rush, season) with **plain-language reasons** for the site engineer.
- The most direct substitute is the phone. SiteSupply has to be faster and less effort than a call, which is why the alert carries the actions and a ready-to-send message.
